A Prior-Data Fitted Tabular Network Framework for Hypertension Screening from Voice Biomarkers

Aires Miguens1, Samuel Chibuoyim Uche2, Emmanuel Agu2

1Data Science Program · 2Department of Computer Science
Worcester Polytechnic Institute, Worcester, MA, USA

Open the poster (PDF) Email the authors

HyperTab, our in-context TabPFN framework, ranks first of 14 models for hypertension screening from voice and demographics. Demographics carry most of the signal; voice alone is weak but real.

Key findings

0.899

HyperTab ranks first of 14 models

ROC-AUC 0.899 (± 0.088) and PR-AUC 0.970 (± 0.020) on all 42 features. CatBoost is closest (0.873); the gap is not statistically significant.

≈ 81%

Demographics dominate the signal

Share of feature importance: age 34.8%, weight 30.0%, height 12.1%, gender 3.9%. Demographics alone (0.913) match the full model (0.899).

0.881

Voice alone is weak but real

ROC-AUC from voice alone with the top 15 voice features; 0.679 with all 38.

Why voice?

About 1.3 billion adults have hypertension and 44% are unaware of it. Cuff-based screening needs equipment and access; speech can be recorded on a phone.

How HyperTab works

  1. Record sustained vowels108 subjects, 58 hypertensive (≥ 130/80 mmHg).
  2. Extract features5,380 windows of 50 ms: 38 voice features + 4 demographics.
  3. Split by subjectLeakage-aware 5-fold cross-validation: train and test never share a subject.
  4. Predict in context (TabPFN)Pretrained on synthetic tables; no gradient updates.
  5. Decide per subjectTop-K mean of window scores, then a threshold.

What it means

  • A pretrained tabular prior is at least on par with tuned boosting on a small cohort.
  • Voice alone is at best a coarse triage signal, not a replacement for clinical assessment.
  • Limits: 108 young subjects, one country. Next: raw audio, larger cohorts, external validation.
